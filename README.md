I'm a middle school student in China, and I want to say this honestly: my technical skills are still limited. I can't patch F-Droid's code, I can't add features, and there are plenty of things in this world of free software that are way above my head. But I've fallen in love with what open source stands for — the idea that software can be free, transparent, and built by strangers helping strangers, just because it's the right thing to do. That idea made me want to give something back, in whatever small way I could. Here's the part I can play: F-Droid's home is on GitLab, and they no longer publish new releases on GitHub. When the official site or GitLab is unreachable — which happens a lot from where I am — people simply have no way to get the latest client. If you can't even install the store, you never get to meet the thousands of FOSS apps behind it. That felt wrong to me, especially for people like me: students, curious kids, anyone just starting out and trying to learn. So this repo is my answer. I mirror every new official signed F-Droid Android client here, exactly as F-Droid builds and signs it — no modifications, no repackaging, ever. Think of it as an extra door: one more place for the people who can't get through the front one. And this isn't a one-time upload. I'm going to maintain it for as long as I can, through flaky networks and busy school days. I can't promise I'll always be fast, but I can promise I'll keep showing up. Each release here is just two files: `F-Droid-*.apk` (the official client) and `F-Droid-*.apk.sha256` (its checksum). Please don't take my word for it — check for yourself. Run `sha256sum -c F-Droid-*.apk.sha256`, and make sure the APK's signature matches F-Droid's official long-lived certificate, SHA-256 `43238d512c1e5eb2d6569f4a3afbf5523418b82e0a3ed1552770abb9a9c9ccab` (documented by F-Droid, valid until 2037). On Linux, `keytool -printcert -jarfile F-Droid-*.apk` should print that same value. Android will also refuse any future update whose signature doesn't match, so verifying the first install keeps you safe from then on. I'm not affiliated with or endorsed by F-Droid. I'm just a kid who got lucky enough to find open source, and who wants more people to find it too. I may be small, my ability may be limited, but this mirror is every bit of effort I've got. If it helps even one person discover free software — one kid who gets curious, one person who learns something new — then every stuck connection and late-night sync was worth it. My dream is just that: to help a few more people get through the door. If you've read this far, maybe you'll pay it forward too, in your own small way. That's how all of us grow. 🤝
